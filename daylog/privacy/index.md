# Daylog Privacy Policy

Effective 6 September 2026

Daylog is a personal life record for iPhone, made by Sarang Studio. It fills each day in from what your phone already knows and lets you add your own words. This policy explains what Daylog reads, where it goes, and what it never does.

## The short version

- Everything Daylog records stays on your iPhone. There is no Daylog account, no Daylog server, and nothing is uploaded to us.
- Daylog contains no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party software.
- Daylog reads from Apple Health, Photos and Location only after you allow each one in iOS's own permission dialogs. Each can be declined or revoked at any time in Settings.
- Your health data is never used for advertising, never sold, and never shared with anyone.

## What Daylog reads on your iPhone

**Apple Health.** With your permission, Daylog reads five things: step count, walking and running distance, sleep, workouts, and your date of birth. It reads only; it never writes to Health. Steps, distance, sleep and workout minutes are stored per day inside the app. Date of birth is used only to show your age alongside the chapters of your life.

**Photos.** With your permission, Daylog reads the date, time, location and count of the photos and videos in your library to work out where you were on each day, going back through your whole library. It stores those facts, not the pictures. Photos and videos are shown inside Daylog directly from your library and are never copied, edited or deleted. Screenshots and photos from shared or synced sources are skipped.

**Location.** With "While Using" permission, Daylog asks iOS once each time you open the app where the phone is, at reduced accuracy, and uses that only to fetch today's weather. The position itself is not stored, and Daylog never tracks where you go.

**Weather.** For today, Daylog looks up the weather where you are; for a past day with a known photo location, it looks up that day's weather there. It stores a one-line summary.

**What you type.** Mood, habits, meals, the people you ate with, chapters of your life and your journal are stored as you enter them.

**Notifications.** If you turn on the daily reminder, iOS schedules a local notification on your phone. Nothing is sent from a server.

## Where your data lives

All of it is stored in Daylog's private storage on your iPhone. It is included in your iPhone backups according to your own iCloud or computer backup settings, and in no other copy. Daylog does not sync between devices.

Deleting Daylog deletes everything it recorded. Individual corrections, such as renaming or removing a place, can be made inside the app.

## Apple services Daylog talks to

Three features send a request to Apple's servers so they can work. In each case the request goes to Apple, not to us, and is covered by [Apple's Privacy Policy](https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/).

- **Naming places.** A coordinate is sent to Apple Maps to turn it into a neighbourhood or city name.
- **Weather.** A coordinate and a date are sent to Apple Weather to fetch that day's conditions. Weather data is provided by Apple Weather; see the [legal attribution page](https://weatherkit.apple.com/legal-attribution.html).
- **City suggestions.** When you type a city while adding a chapter, the text you type is sent to Apple Maps to suggest matches.

No name, account, identifier or health data accompanies any of these requests.

## Health data commitment

In line with Apple's rules for apps that use HealthKit, Daylog never uses health information for advertising, marketing or data mining, never sells it, never shares it with third parties, and never sends it off your phone.

## Children

Daylog is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be published at this address with a new effective date. Because Daylog stores nothing outside your phone, a change here cannot affect data you have already recorded.
